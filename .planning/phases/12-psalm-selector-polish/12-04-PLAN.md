  <decision>
    On the psalm list, small "Book I–V" jump tabs sit on the right edge of the screen so you can hop to Book III without scrolling. Today they only appear on screens narrower than 768px. Turn your phone sideways (844px wide) and they disappear — even though the list of 150 psalms is still far too long to see at once. Which rule should decide whether they show?
  </decision>
  <context>
    Your suggestion was "show them unless about 75% of the page fits on the viewport". That is a reasonable goal, but there are three quite different ways to deliver it, and they behave differently on devices other than the sideways phone you spotted. This choice affects every screen size, not just that one, so it is worth picking deliberately.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="option-a">
      <name>Show the tabs whenever the screen is narrow OR short (recommended)</name>
      <pros>
        A sideways phone is short (390px tall), so the tabs stay — your reported problem is fixed.
        A normal laptop or desktop window is both wide and tall, so the tabs stay hidden exactly as they are today — no change to the desktop experience you already approved.
        Instant: the tabs are simply there or not there the moment the page paints. No flicker, no measuring, nothing to go wrong on a slow connection.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        It is a proxy for "the list does not fit", not a literal measurement. If you drag a desktop browser window down into a short wide strip, the tabs will reappear — arguably correct, but it is a visible change on desktop in that one situation.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="option-b">
      <name>Measure the page and hide the tabs only when the list genuinely fits</name>
      <pros>
        Matches your "75% of the page fits" wording literally. The tabs appear or vanish based on the real amount of content on screen, whatever the device.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        The page has to measure itself after it loads, so on a slow load the tabs can appear a fraction of a second late, or briefly appear then vanish — a small flicker you would notice.
        Has to re-measure on every rotate, resize, search and filter change, and separately inside the psalm-picker pop-up (which scrolls in its own box, not with the page). More moving parts, more places a future change can break it.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="option-c">
      <name>Keep the tabs on any screen up to 1024px wide (instead of 768px)</name>
      <pros>
        The smallest possible change — one number moves. Sideways phones and small tablets keep their tabs.
        Zero new mechanisms, nothing to flicker, essentially no risk of breaking anything else.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        Still a fixed width rule, so it is still guessing. A 900px-wide desktop window at full height comfortably shows the list but would now show the tabs anyway.
        A very wide but very short window (e.g. 1200x400) still loses its tabs.
      </cons>
    </option>
  </options>

  <options>
    <option id="reappears">
      <name>Ticking "Show meter" also shows the tag on closed multi-version boxes (recommended)</name>
      <pros>
        "Show meter" keeps one simple meaning: show me meters, everywhere they apply.
        You can still scan the whole list and see at a glance that Psalm 6 hides a Long Metre version, without opening anything — which is the original reason PSEL-02 was asked for.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        The closed box carries one tag standing in for a whole group, which is slightly less precise than the per-version tags you see once it is open.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="stays-hidden">
      <name>Closed multi-version boxes never show a tag, whatever the checkbox says</name>
      <pros>
        Closed boxes always look identical and uncluttered. Opening a group is the only way meter information ever appears for a multi-version psalm — one rule, no exceptions.
      </pros>
      <cons>
        With "Show meter" ticked, ordinary psalms show their meter but multi-version psalms show nothing, which can read as missing data.
        You lose the at-a-glance "this psalm has a non-CM version" scan entirely, so PSEL-02's original benefit only exists after clicking.
      </cons>
